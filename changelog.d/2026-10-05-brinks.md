### Brinks: paying for the cap-out threat, not just the completion (2026-10-05)

`shadow_explorations.brinks` (schema 25 → 26, private) records every moment a
side reached one flag short of a cap-out, who took that flag, and what followed.

Drew raised it from his own anzio half: the win-probability curve flattens
exactly where the round is about to end, so taking the fourth of five flags
prices at +0.126 and the fifth at +0.102. The **threat** — which turns the enemy
team around and buys space — is worth nothing, while the completion is worth
something. He reached the brink three times in that half, was the credited
capper each time, and the report paid him +0.24, +0.18 and +0.16 as ordinary
flags.

**Measured before built**, against the fair comparison — the same sides taking
one flag fewer, not a random moment, because at a random moment a side is often
already below half and "collapsed" is true before anything happens:

| sample | n | cap-out follows | collapsed | flags at +90s |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| reached N−1 (brink) | 963 | **26.1%** | 43.0% | 2.56 |
| reached N−2 (one short) | 1942 | 13.4% | 46.0% | 2.49 |

Reaching the brink roughly **doubles** the chance of a cap-out inside 90 s —
1.95x against stopping one flag short, 1.58x against a random moment.

**The overextension debit is deliberately absent.** Drew asked for one: a side
that reaches the brink and then loses ground should be punished a little. At team
level the data says the opposite — collapse runs 43.0% after a brink against
46.0% after stopping one short, and the side ends the window holding *more*
flags. There is nothing to debit. The player-level version may well be real (his
push ended with him dead on the enemy's last flag, a cost to him and not to his
team) but that is excursion exposure, which has never beaten chance at any
tightening, so the debit waits for a measurement rather than being invented at
whatever size feels right.

A brink must be **reached from below**: a side that loses a flag from a cap-out,
or that starts a half holding all but one, has not pushed to anything — it is
being pushed. Caught by a test, which is also why the seed comes from the
initial ownership rather than the walk's running state.
