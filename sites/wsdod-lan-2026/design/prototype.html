The World Series of Day of Defeat.

Three days on the basement floor in Chinatown. A ten-company field, 61 players, and 100 matches of Day of Defeat 1.3 — draft Friday, group play Saturday, playoffs and finals Sunday. Every match played on the KTP stack and recorded end to end.

Player stats

HLStatsX + broadcast HUD · tournament (.ktp) matches only · one grid, three views
One formula, three fields. Saturday, Sunday and the full weekend are views of one board, and every view is scored by the formula KTPR actually uses now — six contributions (kill volume, kill efficiency, assists, damage, flags, capture breaks) weighted into one number, each measured against the median of the player’s own position. 1.00 is an average player for the view you’re looking at. What changes when you switch views is only the field being measured against: Saturday is rated against Saturday’s players, Sunday against Sunday’s thinner playoff field, the weekend against combined weekend totals. Same formula everywhere, different baseline populations — so compare numbers within a view, not across views. The weekend view is not an average of the day views: its counts are the two days summed and its rates are recomputed from those totals.
None of this existed last year. Switch to Philly 2025 in the edition bar: same event, same people, and the stats there are a scoreboard transcribed by hand. The difference is two pieces of work built between the LANs. The KTP stack made it possible — a 1.3 server that can be instrumented at all, which is not something DoD came with. The broadcast HUD, written by Cadaver, is what actually watched the matches, and it produced everything the scoreboard never knew: assists, damage down to the hitbox, kill streaks, capture breaks, who spent the round on the deck. One made the recording possible; the other did the recording. Both are open source — github.com/afraznein and github.com/JimmyLockhart65616.
Two sources, and they don't measure the same window. Columns marked HUD come from the broadcast HUD, which saved on a timer while the server was up rather than only during live play — so those figures can include warmup. Two of the six rating inputs are HUD-sourced — assists and capture breaks exist nowhere else — and they carry the badge below. The other four (kill volume, kill efficiency, damage, flags) come from the match record and count match time only. The fix is to teach the match record to log assists and breaks itself; until then the badge is the honest label.
Team finish is deliberately not in here. The live formula can multiply each player by where their company finished. That is switched off for this board — it would fold the result of the weekend into a number presented as individual performance, and the awards this feeds are judged per stage.
About KTPR — an experiment, and how its worked out

Philly LAN 2026 is running an experimental KTPR. It leans on stats that only exist because the broadcast HUD was watching — assists, damage, capture breaks — and that is exactly why it is not the finished article for future seasons. Read it as a worked example of what KTP could rate players on, not a settled standard. It will keep being tweaked.

What it is trying to do. A scoreboard rewards whoever killed the most, and Day of Defeat is not that game. Someone breaks a capture and dies for it. Someone puts four hundred damage into a push a teammate finishes. Someone spends the half holding a flag nobody ends up contesting. Those players win matches and a kill count cannot see any of them. KTPR is an attempt to put a number on the rest of the game, and to weigh each part against what an average player at the same position did — so a sniper is judged against snipers rather than against the field.

Nine seasons of KTPR

This is not a first draft. The three-term formula below is what KTPR has been refined into across roughly nine seasons of KTP play, and it is what the day boards on this page used until now. Every term in it earned its place by being argued over and kept. Three ratios, each against the field average:

KTPR = [ (K/D ÷ field avg K/D) + (kills per half ÷ field avg) + 0.25 × (flags per half ÷ field avg) ] ÷ 2.25

It is honest about what it measures, and what it measures is fragging with a nod to flags — because for nine seasons that was all the server could tell us. A player who never tops a kill count cannot rate well in it, however the match was actually won. The version below is not a replacement for that work so much as the same idea given more to work with: the HUD finally made assists, damage and capture breaks measurable, so they could join the formula rather than be argued about from memory.

What this board uses

Six contributions instead of three, each normalised against the median of the player’s own position, then blended. 1.00 is an average player. Every number below is printed from the weights the engine actually ran:

every stat becomes a ratio to the position median, then is clamped: R = clamp( value ÷ position median, 0.55, 2.5 ) capture breaks get +0.5 on both sides first — the median is near zero, so without it one extra break swamps everything else low kills lift the support terms, because they are saying something the kill count missed: kill term = R(kills) ^ 0.65 damage term = R(damage) × clamp( 1 + 1.2 × (1 − R(kills)), 0.6, 2.5 ) assist term = R(assists) × clamp( 1 + 0.6 × (1 − R(kills)), 0.6, 2.5 ) the blend — kill efficiency carries the most weight: score = ( 1.0 × kill term + 3.0 × R(K/D) + 1.0 × assist term + 0.5 × damage term + 0.7 × R(flags) + 1.0 × R(breaks) ) ÷ 7.2 deaths normalise rather than punish, and dying while out-fragging the field counts as pushing rather than failing: deaths = (deaths per half ÷ position median) ÷ ( 1 + 1.5 × max(0, R(kills) − 1) ) death adjust = 1 − clamp( 0.2 × (deaths − 1), −0.1, +0.15 ) KTPR = score × death adjust

What the LAN bought us

The weekend was the use case. Damage already reaches the match record and this board now reads it from there; assists and capture breaks are the two the record does not log yet, which is the only reason they still carry a HUD badge. The work to log them through HLStatsX is planned for Season 10, and that matters for more than tidiness: the match record is gated to live play, so stats that arrive through it drop warmup automatically the way kills and flags already do. The LAN is what proved the stats were worth having and showed exactly which ones are missing.

Thanks are owed. To Cadaver, for the broadcast HUD — none of the stats beyond a scoreboard exist without it. To Krod and Chi for the work on KTPR itself, which is where the formula above came from. And to everyone else who has put time into KTP’s stats over the years, named here or not — this is a long list and leaving anyone off it would be the wrong kind of accuracy. It is a group effort, and it shows.

Treat HUD columns as close, not exact. On the weekend view, counts are the two days summed, the streak is the better day's run, and rates are recomputed from the summed totals.

Map boards (a map chip on a day view) are rated against that day's baselines with the breaks term omitted — breaks aren't recorded per map — and the remaining five weights renormalized; they also drop the streak/breaks columns and the detail panel, which are day-level. Rules of engagement match rules · complete text

1. General conduct

1.1LAN admins reserve the right to modify any rules as needed at any time as deemed necessary to the good continuation of the tournament.
1.2Matches require the correct map, configuration file, and LAN server. No exceptions.
1.3Server crash protocol. If the server goes down within the first five minutes of a half, the half is restarted in full. After five minutes, the half resumes with score restored and clock rounded up. No substitutions during recovery.
1.4Pixel-walking forbidden. Accessing an area via pixel walking, occupying an area only reachable by pixel walking (e.g., harrington ladder), or hanging in such an area results in suspension and/or forfeiture.
1.5Tied matches proceed to two five-minute overtime halves. Failure to complete overtime rounds results in a loss for both teams.
1.7Seating assignments are final. LAN staff may modify seating as needed for operational reasons.

2. Cheating, penalties & disputes

2.1–2.3Admins investigate suspicious activity. Matches involving cheaters may be overturned.
2.4Prohibited tooling. Visual player-location aids, recoil-control software, target-acquisition software, illegally modified game files, null binds, monitor or software crosshairs, and mouse-acceleration programs.
2.5Only default game files may be used unless explicitly approved or provided by LAN staff. Approved scoreboard modifiers and custom crosshairs (except sniper-rifle crosshair) are allowed.
2.6Spectating after a match goes live is considered cheating. Spec hopping is permitted only during full-map captures or stuck-player situations.

3. Player rules

3.1–3.2Players must pass file and cvar checks, record POV demos each half, screenshot scoreboards, and remain through match conclusion.
3.3Player boosting is allowed so long as the destination area is accessible by a single player without boosting.
3.4Unauthorized voice commands and excessive opponent negging are prohibited.
3.5–3.8Prohibited binds: actions coupled with attack, duck-scrollwheel macros, automated attack or movement scripts, and any weapon-spawning exploits.

4. Captain rules

4.1–4.3Captains: enhance scoreboard screenshot legibility, provide rcon status screenshots, and submit final scores with documentation to the shared drive.

5. Pauses

5.1–5.2Each team receives one two-minute pause per half, callable only immediately after a cap-out.
5.3–5.4Only captains may initiate pauses, with the exception of technical crashes (any team member may call).
5.5Unauthorized-pause penalties: first offense awards opposing team cap-out points; second offense results in immediate match forfeiture.

Code of conduct adult competitors, in good faith · complete text

0. Purpose

—This Code of Conduct exists to ensure a fair, respectful, and enjoyable experience for all participants. As adult competitors, we expect players to uphold the highest standards of integrity, sportsmanship, and professionalism throughout the weekend.

1. Sportsmanship & respect

1.1While toxic behavior is common in this community online, let's strive to do better in person. Treat all players, officials, and spectators with respect. Harassment, abuse, or discriminatory behavior — verbal, written, or physical — will not be tolerated.
1.2Practice good sportsmanship. Healthy competition is encouraged, but win or lose, do so with dignity. Excessive taunting, gloating, or inflammatory behavior is prohibited. A little is normal in this community — keep it within reason.
1.3We are all here to have a good time, connect with others, and be competitive. Be considerate of the people around you.

2. Fair play

2.1No cheating or exploiting of any kind. The LAN follows the KTP rules wherever applicable, including Nein's CVAR checker on all LAN servers.
2.2Use of third-party software, scripts, exploits, or tampering with game files — outside of approved files submitted to LAN organizers prior to the tournament — is grounds for immediate disqualification.

3. Conduct during matches

3.1Follow all match rules and schedules. Be on time and ready to play. Chronic lateness may result in penalties or forfeits. Group-stage schedule will be posted prior to the tournament; knock-out stage prior to leaving for the day. Be responsible for your team and know when your matches will take place.
3.2To keep the tournament on time, matches will start no later than 10 minutes after their scheduled start, regardless of how many players are ready. Teams can choose to forfeit at that time if they don't have a full squad. Ringers are not allowed during tournament play except in extenuating circumstances at tournament-staff discretion.
3.3Be mindful of noise levels and behavior. Keep noise at a reasonable level so everyone can enjoy the gaming experience.
3.4Stream sniping is prohibited. Using information not available to players within their (or their teammates') point of view, in a way that gains unfair advantage, can result in forfeit of the match.
3.5Mumble is the sole authorized comms tool. Using any tool outside of Mumble or in-game voice is prohibited.
3.6Follow instructions from Tournament Staff. They are here to ensure everyone has a good time and to maintain order. Respect their decisions — their decisions are final.
3.7Players are required to use the alias they signed up with, or some variation thereof. Aliasing is prohibited.

4. Conduct outside of matches

4.1Outside of matches, available PCs can be claimed and used at will for other activities. Be aware that PCs are not permanently assigned — be respectful of other attendees and allow others a chance to use the PCs.
4.2No formal time limits on PC use — use your best judgement. Tournament Staff will intervene if necessary to free up PCs for others.

5. Equipment and LAN etiquette

5.1Handle equipment with care. Take care of shared equipment and respect the venue. Treat the space responsibly.
5.2Be accountable for your own equipment. Neither the LAN Center nor Tournament Staff is responsible for any lost or stolen items. Bring a tote or box for your peripherals when not actively using a PC.
5.3Clean up your area after using a PC — including when leaving for an extended period — so that others may have a chance to play. Ask Tournament Staff before leaving peripherals at a specific PC.
5.4Be respectful of, and do not tamper with, others' equipment.
5.5Use the network responsibly. Don't abuse bandwidth or download anything illegal.
5.6Report any damaged or malfunctioning equipment to Tournament Staff immediately.
5.7Admin access is available on the PCs, but limit any changes to those necessary for your peripheral use. All changes are subject to review by Tournament Staff. This includes "Snap Tap." No unauthorized software or hardware modifications. If unsure, ask an official before making changes.

6. Substance use

6.1We are here to have a good time — maintain control of yourself when consuming substances. Excessive rowdiness can result in expulsion from the LAN for the remainder of the day.
6.2The LAN center has banned smoking inside. Respect their rules.
6.3Vaping is allowed by the LAN center, but be considerate of others — keep excessive vaping outside.

7. Penalties for violations

7.1Tournament organizers reserve the right to issue the following based on severity and frequency of misconduct: verbal or written warnings; match or round forfeiture; temporary or permanent removal from the tournament; ban from future events.

8. Agreement

8.1By participating in this tournament, you agree to abide by this Code of Conduct. Ignorance of the rules is not a valid defense. Let's work together to foster a competitive but respectful community.
